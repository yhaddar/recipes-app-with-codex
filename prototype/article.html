The long way around the loaf.

On patience, good butter, and why the best recipes leave a little room for the day you’re having.

NOWords by Nora Okafor · November 3, 2026

There is a particular hush that arrives when dough is left to do its own work. The kitchen does not become empty; it becomes observant. A bowl on the counter, a cloth tucked around it, a promise that will be ready later.

I used to think a recipe was a set of instructions to obey. Now I think of it as an invitation to pay attention. The weather changes the proof. The flour has a point of view. Your hands know when the dough has become itself.

Leave a little room

The best kitchen systems do the same thing. They give us enough structure to begin, but leave room for the week we are actually living. A saved recipe can be a plan, a memory, or just a soft place to start.

So make the loaf. Take the long way around. Let dinner become a reason to stay a little longer.
